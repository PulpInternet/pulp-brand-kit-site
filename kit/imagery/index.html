Imagery

Symbols, plates, prints, glass, icons and photos, with exact values.

Texture by position

typodojo uses ink prints and wave bands. Pulp uses dithered plates and Pulp prints, one at a time. Thinkwell uses none, because the product is the picture.

Symbols

Every symbol is drawn in the gum robot's hand: its cloud, its line (7 units at 640 wide, swelling and tapering like ink), its sparkles, and one object poking out where the robot's gum would be. Ink and ground only. A dashed line means modeled or simulated.

Image scale

Images stay smaller than the words. Beside text an image is at most half the reading column and never taller than the text block next to it.

Icons

One line set on a 24 grid, 1.6 stroke, round caps, no fill, drawn in currentColor. Files are in assets/icons/.

Photos

Real people are never dithered. Every photo has a credit and a license, and every photo of a person has consent on file. Pulp photographs night, light and people talking, in night blues, violets and sodium golds that sit with Halftone and Highlighter. They run full bleed, as up to three bands between sections and behind the one feature block, with a caption on the scrim. Each is WebP at 640, 1280 and 1920 px, lazy, sized and painted first as a 24 px placeholder. Mono turns them gray through --c-photo-filter.

Gradients

Three, all tokens, all light. A gradient runs between two roles of one mode and text sits only on its solid end.

Token What it does Range
--grad-hero A glow of --c-glow behind the robot; the ground settles into the band glow 8 to 28%, set at 22%
--grad-scrim The photo ground rising under a caption 60 to 90%, set at 82%
--grad-feature The feature color solid, then thinning over the photo; 180deg on phones, 90deg from 1024 px solid 30 to 45%, set at 34%

Depth

Parallax is scroll-driven CSS (animation-timeline: view()). A photo travels 24 percent of the screen height (40 to 240 px) against its band, about two thirds of the scroll speed; words never move. Where a browser lacks scroll timelines, a few lines of script do the same. With reduced motion, or without scroll timelines, it stands still.

Rules

ID Scope Checked by Rule
R-GRD-01 pulp lint Three gradients, all tokens: --grad-hero (a glow of the mode's --c-glow, 8 to 28 percent, over a ground that settles into the band), --grad-scrim (the photo ground rising 60 to 90 percent under a caption) and --grad-feature (the feature color solid for its first 30 to 45 percent, then thinning over a photo).
R-GRD-02 pulp review A gradient is light, not a color of its own. It runs between two roles of the same mode, never across two hues, and text sits only on its solid end.
R-PHO-01 pulp review Photos are free to use under a license recorded in assets/source/photos.json with the photographer and the source page. Each band credits its photographer by name only in its caption (R-IMG-12), the feature block in its corner, and the footer lists them all, with no license line on the page. Originals stay out of git.
R-PHO-02 pulp review What Pulp photographs: night, light and people in conversation or in crowds, never posed stock, never a screen of a product, never a face we cannot credit. Night blues, violets and sodium golds, so they sit with Halftone and Highlighter.
R-PHO-03 pulp layout Photos run full bleed in four places: behind the hero, under --grad-hero so the type sits on its solid end; as bands between sections; as a break inside a section, three quarters of a band's height; and behind the one feature block. At most three bands and breaks a page, never two touching, never in a card or beside text. A band or break caption sits on the scrim, in Newsprint, one line at heading size. The hero photo has no caption and is credited in the footer.

R-PHO-05 pulp lint Photos follow the mode through --c-photo-filter: full color in light and dark, grayscale in mono and on typodojo.
R-PHO-06 pulp layout Parallax you can see: a photo travels 24 percent of the screen height against its band (40 to 240 px) as the band crosses the screen, so it moves at about two thirds of the scroll. Scroll-driven CSS first, a per-frame script only where scroll timelines are missing. Never on text, never on a plate. With reduced motion the photo stands still.

R-GLS-04 house review Use glass for things that float over a print, a plate or scrolling content: menus, pickers, the bar, a call to action over a print. Never as a card, and never under more than three lines of body text.
R-GLS-05 house review Without backdrop-filter the veil goes to 92 percent. With reduced transparency it is the solid ground. In forced colors it is Canvas.
R-GLS-06 house review By position: typodojo veil 62, blur 16, saturation 1.2, as in its mode picker; Pulp veil 72, blur 14, no saturation boost; Thinkwell no glass panes, only modal scrims blurred 8 to 12 px.
R-PRT-01 pulp review Pulp has sixteen prints, in text, geometric and brand families. Each has one job, listed in the kit.
R-PRT-02 pulp lint A print is one ink, var(--c-print), at 1.08 to 1.25:1 against what it sits on: a texture you notice second, never a picture. Never an accent, never Highlighter.
R-PRT-03 pulp review At most two prints on a page, as strips between sections or one strip inside the feature block. Two strips never touch, and a print never sits behind a plate, a symbol or the robot.
R-PRT-04 pulp layout A print tile shows 800 to 1600 px wide. Smaller reads as noise; crop it instead.
R-PRT-05 pulp review A print never sits behind text. Text prints are read as texture, so body text over them is noise. Pulp prints do not move; typodojo's may.
R-PRT-07 pulp layout A print strip is a band of print with no text, 96 px tall on phones and 120 px from 768, set between sections to change the pace.
R-PRT-06 pulp review Text prints use the whole wordmark or a phrase from the promise, set in Libre Franklin 800 and outlined. Never a letter of the wordmark alone. The Wordmark run drops the trademark sign, which stays on the single wordmark in the bar.
